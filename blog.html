The Catalyst: Why Go Back to School?

For over three years, I worked as a UAV Systems Engineer at F-drones, taking autonomous aerial platforms like the Hypercopter and Hyperlaunch from early MVP stages to commercial deployment. It was an incredible proving ground. We were dealing with the messy reality of hardware integration, sensor fusion tuning, and landing VTOLs on moving vessels.

Before that, my foundation was built during my undergraduate years in Mechanical Engineering at MIT World Peace University, where I spent four years designing heavy-lift fixed-wing aircraft and leading our SAE Aero Design team, The Skytroopers. These experiences taught me how to be resourceful, build rapidly, and produce results under strict constraints.

But they also highlighted a critical gap: scaling a robotic system from a functional prototype to a robust, market-ready product requires more than just good hardware and software. It requires a holistic, systems-level approach to architecture, business viability, and advanced autonomy. I realized that to build the next generation of complex robotic systems—and to take them from concept to deployment reliably—I needed to deepen my expertise in systems engineering.

Why CMU's MRSD Program?

When looking at graduate programs, Carnegie Mellon University's Master of Science in Robotic Systems Development (MRSD) stood out immediately.

Unlike traditional, purely research-focused degrees, MRSD operates at the exact intersection of advanced technical development and business strategy. It is designed for engineers who want to understand the entire product lifecycle. The program perfectly aligns with my goal: gaining the ability to Conceptualize, Architect, Design, Build, Test, Validate, and Deploy highly complex systems.

I didn't just want to write better control algorithms or design stronger airframes in isolation; I wanted the framework to ensure all those pieces integrate into a system that actually solves a real-world problem.

What Excites Me the Most Right Now

The most thrilling part of being at the CMU Robotics Institute is the sheer scale and ambition of the work happening around me. I am currently working on my capstone project at the AIR Lab, titled the "Flying Hand."

We are developing a fully-actuated multirotor designed to perform precise non-destructive testing (NDT) for industrial assets. Instead of just flying near an object to inspect it with a camera, we are building a platform capable of stable hover while exerting 6-DoF wrenches to physically interact with the environment. It pushes the boundaries of fly-by-wire interaction controllers and state estimation, and it perfectly bridges my background in UAVs with advanced manipulation.

The Horizon: What I Wish to Get Out of This

Ultimately, I want to leave CMU with the architectural foresight to lead cross-functional robotics teams.

Growing up with an ENT surgeon and an oncologist in my immediate family, I have always been surrounded by conversations about medical innovation and the critical need for precision and reliability. That lifelong exposure is a massive driving force for me. Whether it is a fully-actuated drone inspecting a critical industrial asset or a highly precise robotic system assisting in an operating room, the underlying challenge is the same: bridging complex robotics with environments where failure is not an option.

MRSD is giving me the tools to not just build the technology, but to guarantee its reliability and deploy it where it matters most.
